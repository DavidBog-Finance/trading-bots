Trading Bots & Agents Playbook

An always-on trading setup that runs on your MacBook and reports to your iPhone. Three tested, once-a-day rules bots (S&P 500, Bitcoin, SPY put spreads) form the steady core. Two Claude agents, one for stocks and one for crypto, watch the whole market and every headline all day, wake up when something happens, and decide what to buy or sell within hard limits written in code. Your phone buzzes only when trades have filled.

The bottom line

I tested about a dozen well-known strategies on 98 years of S&P 500 data, 13 years of Bitcoin data and 35 years of VIX data, with fees and realistic delays. Three designs held up, and those are your bots. Each one sizes itself as a share of your account, so every deposit gets put to work on the next run without touching any settings.

What these bots really do. None of them is a money machine. The stock bot earned less than simply holding SPY since 2000; what it buys you is a much smaller worst-case loss. The crypto bot roughly matched Bitcoin's return with less than half the crash. The options bot harvests a small, steady premium and occasionally gives a chunk back. At $1,000 a good year is pocket money; the payoff is a tested system that grows with your deposits without blowing up.

Anyone selling you a bot that makes 5% a week is selling the bot, not the returns. The research below explains why the honest numbers look like this.

Day trading, options and crypto rules

The $25,000 day-trading rule is being retired

On April 14, 2026 the SEC approved FINRA's replacement of the Pattern Day Trader rule. The $25,000 minimum and the "4 day trades in 5 days" count are replaced by intraday margin checks. Two catches: brokers get up to 18 months to switch over, so yours may still enforce the old rule today, and margin still needs $2,000 of equity. A cash account was never subject to PDT, but you can only trade with settled money (stock trades settle the next business day).

Why this doesn't matter for your bots: all three trade at most once a day and hold overnight, so none is a day-trading strategy. That is deliberate (see the evidence section). Once your account passes $2,000 and Alpaca moves to the new rule, you could day trade freely, but the evidence says you shouldn't.

Options

Alpaca supports options through the API at $0 commission, including multi-leg spreads once you're approved for options Level 3 (apply in the Alpaca dashboard; spreads generally need a margin account, which needs $2,000+ equity). Buying options is where retail traders lose most: studies of the 2019–2021 options boom found large aggregate losses, mostly to bid-ask spreads and overpriced lottery-ticket calls. Selling index puts is the side with decades of evidence behind it, so that's what Bot 3 does, with a bought put underneath to cap the loss.

Crypto

Crypto trades 24/7 with no PDT rule, which is why the crypto bot uses UTC calendar days. US residents can't use offshore leverage or perpetual futures on major exchanges, and the bot uses no leverage anyway.

What the evidence says

I looked for rules with long, independent track records and good economic reasons to work. I also looked at what has consistently failed for retail traders.

Two warnings shaped every decision. McLean & Pontiff (2016) found published strategies earn about 26% less out-of-sample and 58% less after publication. Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu (2014) showed that if you try enough parameter combinations, you will always find a backtest that looks amazing and means nothing. So I fixed the test periods before looking, preferred broad parameter plateaus over sharp peaks, and averaged several lookbacks instead of picking the best one.

How I tested

  • Data. S&P 500 daily closes and total return (dividends included, ETF expense ratio applied) plus T-bill rates, 1928–2025. Bitstamp BTC/USD minute bars, 2012 to Oct 21, 2026, rebuilt into daily bars.
  • Split. Stocks: tune on 1928–1999, judge on 2000–2025. Crypto: tune on 2013–2020, judge on 2021–2026.
  • Costs. Stocks 0.05% per trade (SPY's spread is about 0.01%, so this is conservative). Crypto 0.30% per trade (Alpaca's 0.25% taker fee + slippage), and stress-tested at 0.50%.
  • Timing. Decide on the close, trade at that close. Re-tested with a full one-day delay: results barely changed, so the edge doesn't depend on perfect timing.
  • Walk-forward. Re-tuning the stock trend length every 5 years on the prior 20 years gave 11.14%/yr, the same as just using 200 days (11.21%). Re-tuning added nothing, so the bots use fixed, standard settings.

Bot 1: SPY trend + pullback

Trades: SPY only (optionally parks idle cash in SGOV, a T-bill ETF). When: once a day, about 20–40 minutes before the 4:00 pm ET close (1:20 pm Pacific).

Sleeve A: trend (50%)

  • Hold SPY when it closes more than 1% above its 200-day average.
  • Go to cash when it closes more than 1% below.
  • In between, keep what you have. This band halves whipsaw trades.

Sleeve B: RSI(2) pullback (50%)

  • Buy when SPY is above its 200-day average and the 2-day RSI is under 10 (a sharp short dip).
  • Sell when SPY closes above its 5-day average (usually 2–5 days later).
  • Invested about 10% of the time.

Bot 2: BTC trend ensemble

Trades: BTC/USD only. When: once a day just after 00:00 UTC (5 pm Pacific in summer, 4 pm in winter).

  1. Trend vote. Six yes/no checks: is BTC above its 50, 100 and 150-day averages, and is its 28, 56 and 120-day return positive? The score is the share of "yes" votes (0 to 1).
  2. Size by volatility. Multiply the score by 40% ÷ BTC's recent 30-day annualized volatility, capped at 100%. BTC usually runs 50–80% volatility, so a full "yes" means holding roughly half to all of the allocation.
  3. Trade only on real changes. Rebalance when the target moves 10 points away from what you hold, or when it drops to zero. That cut trading from about 230 to about 74 small trades a year.

Bot 3: SPY put credit spreads

Trades: SPY options, one spread position at a time. When: right after the stock bot, ~1:20 pm Pacific. Off by default (OPTIONS_ENABLED=false) until you're approved for Level 3 and have paper-tested it.

  1. Only in uptrends. Opens a new spread only when the stock bot's 200-day trend sleeve is ON.
  2. Sell the 25-delta put about 45 days out (roughly 5–7% below SPY's price, about a 1-in-4 chance of finishing in the money), and buy a put about 3% of SPY's price lower (about $20 below at today's prices). You collect a credit up front.
  3. Get out early. Close when 50% of the credit is captured, or at 21 days to expiry, whichever comes first. That avoids the last weeks when losses accelerate and keeps assignment risk low.
  4. Size by worst case. The most one position can lose is the strike gap minus the credit, about $1,600 per spread today. The bot only opens as many spreads as fit inside OPTIONS_RISK_PER_TRADE (20%) of the options sleeve. If one doesn't fit, it waits and tells you how much equity it needs.

How I tested it, and why the range is wide

I couldn't get historical option quotes, so I priced every spread with Black-Scholes using that day's VIX, adjusted for put skew, plus $0.10 of slippage per spread each way. Results depend heavily on the skew assumption, so I report a range rather than one number. Narrow spreads ($5 wide) lost money in most versions once costs were included; only wide spreads held up in every version.

Options details to know

  • Assignment: SPY options are American-style, so a short put can be assigned early if it's deep in the money. Closing at 21 days makes this rare. If it happens, you'd briefly own 100 SPY shares per contract; the long put still caps the loss. Close everything in the Alpaca app and the bot carries on.
  • Order pricing: the bot sends one multi-leg limit order at the mid price. Alpaca's docs don't spell out whether a credit is a negative limit price, so OPTIONS_CREDIT_IS_NEGATIVE is a setting. Check your first paper fill and flip it if the order is rejected.
  • Earnings and events don't apply to SPY, but big macro days (Fed, CPI) move it. The bot doesn't try to dodge them.

Claude agents: any stock, any coin, reacting to news

A bot follows a fixed recipe. An agent gets a goal, tools and limits, looks at what is happening, and decides. Both agents here are Claude, running on your Mac through Claude Code and your Claude Pro plan. Each run, the agent:

  1. Gets woken upOn a schedule (stocks: 10:00, 12:30 and 15:10 New York time; crypto: every 6 hours) or the moment something happens: a material headline about something it holds, a strongly positive one about something it could buy, a holding moving 5% in a day (8% for crypto), SPY falling 2%, BTC moving 5%, or a hack/delisting story.
  2. Looks aroundIts tools: its portfolio with the thesis it wrote when buying, a whole-market scan (about 1,000–1,500 liquid US stocks and ETFs ranked by momentum and news, plus today's top movers; or every Alpaca coin ranked by trend and news), detailed lookups on any symbol, and the scored news feed. It also reads its own last few journal entries, so it remembers what it was thinking.
  3. Decides and actsBuys or sells with a written reason, or does nothing. Every order passes a risk gate in code before it reaches Alpaca.
  4. ReportsIf it traded, your phone gets one push after the orders fill: what it bought or sold, the fill prices, and its reasoning. If it decided to do nothing, you hear nothing.

The news feed. Every Alpaca/Benzinga headline (stocks and crypto, around the clock) is scored by Claude for each ticker it names: direction (−1 to +1), how much it matters (0 to 1), and a tag such as earnings, acquisition target, guidance, dilution, hack. Scores tilt the market ranking, block acquisition targets and bankruptcies, and trigger the wake-ups above.

What the evidence says about this

Be clear-eyed: the agents can't be backtested, the rules bots can. The ingredients have research behind them. Momentum across stocks has decades of out-of-sample evidence (Jegadeesh & Titman 1993; volatility-scaled momentum, Barroso & Santa-Clara 2015). Lopez-Lira & Tang found that LLM scores of news headlines predict next-day stock returns, strongest in smaller stocks and fading within days. But a 2025 study (Gao, Jiang & Yan) showed LLM backtests are inflated because the model may remember what happened next; the honest tests use only news from after the model's training cutoff, where the effect was smaller but still there in 2024. Nobody can backtest an agent like this one honestly, so the only real test is paper trading it forward. Expect it to be noisier than the rules bots, and judge it after 3+ months against simply holding SPY and BTC.

Which Claude it uses

Default: your Claude Pro plan (CLAUDE_BACKEND=pro). The runner calls Claude Code in its non-interactive mode (claude -p), signed in with your account. No API key, no extra bill. Claude Code's own tools (commands, files, web) are switched off for these calls, so Claude can only answer; every action goes through the bot's risk-checked tools. Each news batch is one call, and each agent run is about 3–8 calls.

  • Shared limits. These calls count against the same usage limits as your own Claude chats and Claude Code. When the limit is hit, the agents and news scoring pause for an hour and try again; the rules bots and stop-losses keep running without Claude. The status command shows how many calls were made today.
  • Model. Blank CLAUDE_CODE_MODEL uses your plan's default. Set opus for the strongest reasoning (uses your limits faster), or haiku to go easy on them.
  • Plan terms. A program calling Claude on its own around the clock is a heavier, more automated use than a person chatting. Check Anthropic's current terms for your plan. If it ever becomes a problem, switch to the API.

Alternative: the Claude API (CLAUDE_BACKEND=api plus an ANTHROPIC_API_KEY from platform.claude.com). It's billed per token, separately from your plan, and it doesn't touch your chat limits. Rough cost for a normal day (about 11 agent runs plus news):

Keeping a laptop awake

  • Plugged in, lid open. The installer runs sudo pmset -c sleep 0 (never sleep on the charger; the screen still turns off) and wraps the runner in caffeinate. A MacBook still sleeps when you close the lid unless an external display is attached, so leave it open (screen dims on its own). Or check System Settings → Battery → Options → Prevent automatic sleeping on power adapter when the display is off.
  • Restarts. launchd starts the runner after you log in. macOS updates can restart overnight: turn on automatic login if FileVault allows it, or log in after an update. pmset autorestart brings the Mac back after a power cut.
  • Know when it's down. A sleeping Mac can't tell you it's asleep. Create a free check at healthchecks.io (period 5 min, grace 10 min), connect its ntfy or email alert, and paste the ping URL into HEALTHCHECK_URL. If the Mac goes quiet, you get a push. The runner also tells you when it wakes after a gap.
  • Missed runs are safe. A rules bot that misses its window simply trades the next day. The crypto bot catches up the same day. Stop-losses resume the moment the Mac is back; Alpaca holds your positions either way.
  • Battery health. macOS's Optimized Battery Charging holds a plugged-in MacBook around 80%, so being on the charger 24/7 is fine.

More trades, more coins

More trades only help if each one has an edge after fees. Shortening lookbacks or trading intraday made every strategy worse in testing. The honest way to trade more is to run the same tested rules on more markets:

  • Stocks: set STOCK_RSI2_SYMBOLS=SPY,QQQ,IWM,DIA. The RSI(2) pullback rule is widely reported to work on all four index ETFs, and each adds ~15 trades a year. The pullback half of the stock budget is split evenly across them.
  • Crypto: set CRYPTO_SYMBOLS=BTC/USD,ETH/USD (and others Alpaca lists). The crypto budget is split evenly across coins, and each gets the same trend and volatility rules. Smaller coins are more volatile, so the bot automatically holds less of them.

I couldn't download ETF or altcoin history from my environment, so I built validate.py. On your Mac it downloads each symbol from Yahoo Finance and runs the unchanged rules. Because the rules were fixed on SPY and BTC first, every other symbol is a true out-of-sample test. It prints the exact settings line to paste, including only symbols that passed (stocks: Sharpe above 0.4 and return above 3%/yr; coins: Sharpe above 0.5 and a smaller drawdown than holding). Make that your first Claude Code task.

Adding money

  • Nothing to change. Each bot sizes itself as a share of total equity, so a deposit is used on the next run. The stock bot ignores drift under 5% so small deposits don't cause trades every day; crypto tops up when a coin is 10 points under target.
  • The loss limit follows your deposits. The bots read your deposit history from Alpaca. If equity falls more than MAX_LOSS_PCT (35%) below what you've put in, all bots sell and stop. Depositing more raises the limit automatically.
  • Deposit by ACH in the Alpaca app (free). New money can take a few days to be withdrawable, and in a cash account it must settle before the bots can trade with it.
  • Dollar-cost averaging is built in. Regular deposits buy in at many prices, which smooths your entry no matter what the bots are doing.
  • Want a hard ceiling? Set STOCK_MAX_USD, CRYPTO_MAX_USD or OPTIONS_MAX_USD. Anything above stays in cash.

Taxes: federal + California

General information, not tax advice. With amounts this small the dollars are tiny, but the paperwork isn't, so get it right from day one.

How your gains are taxed

  • Short-term (held 1 year or less): almost every bot trade. Taxed as ordinary income. Federal 2026 single brackets: 10% to $12,400, 12% to $50,400, 22% to $105,700, 24% to $201,775. No FICA/payroll tax on trading gains.
  • California taxes all capital gains as ordinary income, short or long term: 1% to 9.3% for most incomes (9.3% from about $75,200 taxable), up to 13.3% above $1M.
  • Example (illustrative income): at $60,000 taxable income you're in the 22% federal and 8% California brackets, so each $100 of bot profit costs about $30 in tax.
  • Losses offset gains first; beyond that, up to $3,000 a year can offset wages (federal and California), and the rest carries forward.

Wash sales (stocks only)

If you sell SPY at a loss and buy SPY again within 30 days, the loss is disallowed for now and added to the cost of the new shares. The RSI(2) sleeve will trigger this regularly. You don't lose the deduction permanently; it's deferred until you finally sell. Alpaca's 1099-B tracks it for you. Watch late December: a loss sale followed by a January rebuy pushes that loss into the next tax year.

Options

  • SPY options are equity options, so gains and losses are short-term capital gains (ordinary income rates), reported on your 1099-B. Index options like SPX and XSP get a 60% long-term / 40% short-term split under Section 1256, but Alpaca doesn't offer index options.
  • A spread that's closed counts as two trades (one per leg). Assignment folds the option's premium into the stock's cost basis.
  • Wash-sale rules can apply between options and the underlying ETF (for example, a loss on SPY shares with a new short put on SPY within 30 days). Alpaca's 1099-B handles what it can; tax software imports it.

Crypto

  • The IRS treats crypto as property. Every sale, including a partial rebalance, is a taxable event.
  • The wash-sale rule does not apply to crypto as of October 2026. Several bills have proposed changing that; none has passed.
  • Form 1099-DA: for 2025 trades, brokers reported gross proceeds only. For crypto bought on or after January 1, 2026 and kept at the same broker, they also report cost basis. Keep your crypto at Alpaca so your basis stays "covered".
  • Answer "Yes" to the digital-asset question on Form 1040, and report trades on Form 8949 / Schedule D, plus California Schedule D (540).

What to do

  1. Every spring, download Alpaca's consolidated 1099 (stocks) and 1099-DA (crypto) and import them into tax software. TurboTax, FreeTaxUSA and others accept these files.
  2. Expect roughly 15–60 stock trades, 75+ small crypto trades per coin, and up to 36 option legs a year. The import handles volume; you don't type them.
  3. If bot profits ever exceed about $1,000 a year, raise your W-4 withholding at work a little to avoid an underpayment penalty. California follows the same idea.
  4. A Roth IRA would avoid tax entirely, but Alpaca's API doesn't support IRAs; IBKR does. Consider it once you're managing real money.

Working with Claude Code

Open a terminal in the folder and run claude. The repo includes a CLAUDE.md that tells Claude the house rules: one source of truth for signals, judge changes only on out-of-sample data, keep the safety rails, run tests before committing. Prompts that work well:

  • "Run validate.py and set my symbols to the ones that passed."
  • "Explain what strategies.py does line by line, like I'm new to Python."
  • "Run all three bots in dry-run mode and explain today's decisions."
  • "Check my last options paper fill: did the credit sign work? Fix OPTIONS_CREDIT_IS_NEGATIVE if not."
  • "Switch the crypto bot to limit orders at the mid price that fall back to market after 2 minutes. Add tests. Show me the fee savings in a backtest."
  • "Add a weekly push comparing my P&L to just holding SPY and BTC."
  • "Once my paper account has 3 months of options trades, compare actual fills to the model's assumed $0.10 slippage."
  • "The Actions run failed. Read the log and fix it."
  • "Grade the agents: compare each agent's trades this month with what the momentum ranking alone would have done, and with holding SPY/BTC."
  • "The stock agent keeps buying stocks that are already up 20% on the day. Tighten its instructions and add a test."
  • "Show me what Claude cost this month by agent and model, and what switching news scoring to Haiku would save."

Be suspicious of any change that improves the in-sample backtest a lot. Ask Claude: "What's the out-of-sample result, and how many variations did we try?"

Going live checklist

Go live only when all of these are true. Your roadmap targets month 4.

  • 3+ months of paper trading with no failed runs you didn't understand, including at least a few closed option spreads.
  • Paper trades match what the backtest logic says should have happened on those days.
  • You've read the tax section and know where your 1099s will come from.
  • You have an emergency fund separate from this money.
  • Agents: you've read their weekly journals, their trades made sense, and their paper results beat or roughly matched holding SPY/BTC after Claude costs. If not, set AGENT_STOCK_PCT=0 / AGENT_CRYPTO_PCT=0 and keep them on paper.

Then: fund the live Alpaca account by ACH. Create live API keys and put them in .env on the Mac (and the GitHub secrets, for the backup). Set ALPACA_PAPER=false, I_UNDERSTAND_LIVE_RISK=yes, and delete NET_DEPOSITS_OVERRIDE (live accounts read your real deposits). Turn on options only after Level 3 approval and once your equity passes the threshold for your chosen risk setting. Deposit as you like from then on; the bots scale with you.

Stop or rethink if: the stock bot underperforms buy-and-hold by more than 15 points in a rising market for 2 years, the crypto bot's drawdown exceeds −45%, or the options sleeve loses more than 40% (all worse than anything in testing). Each means the market changed or something is broken. For the agents: pause them if a sleeve loses more than 30% or trails holding SPY/BTC by 15+ points over 6 months.
