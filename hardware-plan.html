Gigabyte Aero 14 + M3 MacBook Pro · October 2026

Home Server Plan

The trading bots use a tiny slice of the Aero. Here's what else the two machines can do, ordered by how much it's worth to you, keeping both cool and quiet.

The short version. Turn the Aero into a quiet home server on Ubuntu, reachable from your iPhone and Mac through Tailscale. Put its spare capacity to work in this order:

  1. Make the trading smarter: a local market-data store, a scorecard that checks whether Claude's news scores and the agents actually make money, and extra strategies tested on paper.
  2. Run local AI as a backup brain and a filter, not as the decision-maker. The GTX 1060 runs small models well, and the 64 GB of RAM can hold a mid-size one.
  3. Useful home services: phone photo backup, Mac backups, ad blocking for the house, a media server and game streaming.

Skip crypto mining: it loses money on LA electricity.

Where it helps the bots

  • Backup when you hit your Pro limit. Today the news watcher and agents pause for an hour when the limit is reached. A local model can keep scoring headlines in the meantime, so nothing material slips by. Trades stay with Claude.
  • Pre-filter the news. A local model drops the noise (stock lists, recycled stories, "3 stocks to watch") before anything goes to Claude. Your Pro usage then goes to headlines that matter and to the agents' thinking.
  • Duplicate detection. A small embedding model spots the same story from five sources, so it's scored once.
  • Second opinion, measured. Run a local-model agent as a shadow strategy on paper and let the scorecard decide whether it's any good.

Everyday uses

  • A private chat assistant on your iPhone through Open WebUI and Tailscale, for anything you don't want to leave the house.
  • Voice memos to text with Whisper, which runs well on the GTX 1060.
  • Searching your own documents and notes with a local index.

GTX 1060 specifics. Use NVIDIA's 580 driver branch, the last one that supports Pascal cards, with security fixes promised through Oct 2028. Use CUDA 12-based builds, since CUDA 13 dropped Pascal. Ollama still lists the card as supported. Keep it at a 75–80 W power limit (nvidia-smi -pl 80): you lose almost no AI speed, and it runs cooler and quieter.

Rule that stays: local models never place trades. They score, filter and summarize; Claude and the code's risk limits decide.

Skip these

IdeaWhy not
Crypto mining on the GTX 1060It earns pennies a day. Running the card around the clock draws roughly 2–2.5 kWh, about $0.60–0.75 a day at LA rates, so you lose money and wear out the card.
Folding@home / BOINC at full loadWorthy, but it pins the CPU and GPU at 100% around the clock. That's the opposite of not pushing it.
Opening ports to the internetAn old laptop that holds your trading keys shouldn't be reachable from the internet. Use Tailscale only.
Local models making tradesToo weak for that, and it would undo the safety design. Keep them as helpers.
Running the bots on Windows 10Updates run out in Oct 2027 even with the extension, and Windows can restart itself. Use Ubuntu.

Keeping it gentle

  • Cap the GPU at 75–80 W, and leave the CPU on the powersave governor. Heavy jobs then just take a bit longer.
  • Schedule heavy work for 9 am–5 pm (research, model jobs) while you're at work. The Aero shares your bedroom, so nights stay silent with only the bots running.
  • Watch temperatures. A small script pushes to ntfy if the CPU stays above 85 °C or the GPU above 80 °C. Clean the fans first; nine years of dust is the most likely cause of heat.
  • Protect the data. Check the SSD's health monthly (the 970 EVO 1 TB is rated for 600 TB written, far beyond this workload). Back up .env and the bots' memory file somewhere safe.
  • Power. A small UPS ($60–90) rides out short blackouts, and the server comes back on its own after long ones. Deal with the old battery as covered in Aero 14 vs M3 MacBook Pro.
  • Electricity. Bots plus home services: ~15–30 W average, about $40–80 a year. Add $10–30 a year for regular local-AI use.

What the MacBook does

It becomes your control center: Claude Code to change the bots, the dashboard and Open WebUI in a browser, and the faster machine for local models while you're at it. Nothing important depends on it being home, awake or charged.

Suggested order

  1. Week 1: Ubuntu, Tailscale, the bots as a systemd service, temperature and uptime alerts, Time Machine target.
  2. Week 2: local market-data store and the news and agent scorecard.
  3. Week 3: Ollama on the GTX 1060 as a news pre-filter and a backup when you hit your Pro limit.
  4. Any time after: AdGuard Home, Immich, game streaming and the rest, one at a time.

I can build the Week 1–3 pieces into the bot code: the Linux installer, the scorecard, the data store and the local-model backup.
